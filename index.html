Our mission

The hard part isn't wanting it. It's finding a way in.

Plenty of students want to work in medicine. Far fewer have someone to show them how. MedXplore exists to close that gap — with real guidance, real people, and a room where it's safe to be a beginner.

We aim to inspire and mentor any student with a strong interest in health care, regardless of prior education or experience, and to connect them with professionals, organizations, and institutions along the way.

See how we do it

“Our mission is to get rid of obstacles in the medical field while offering students opportunities to prepare them for the future.”

— MedXplore
  • Not enough chances. Opportunities in medicine cluster around students who already have connections.
  • Not enough resources. The material is out there, but nobody tells you where to start or what actually matters.
  • Not enough mentors. Students early in high school are left to guess, right when guidance would change the most.

What we do

Four ways we get students closer to medicine

Everything MedXplore runs is built around one idea: exposure early, and exposure often.

Live science sessions

Small, peer-led classes that unpack the biology behind medicine — cells, energetics, respiration, genetics — taught the way we wish someone had taught us. Each session runs in two batches so schedules aren't a barrier.

The curriculum

What a MedXplore session actually looks like

Our current series builds from a single cell up to the genes that run it. Every session is live, discussion-friendly, and runs twice — pick the batch that fits your week. Slides below are pulled straight from the real classes.

Session 01

Cells & cellular organization

Where all of biology starts. We work up the ladder from a single cell to the biosphere, then look at why a single cell type failing can bring the whole system down with it.

  • Levels of organization
  • Tissues & organs
  • Organ systems
  • Populations & ecosystems
  • Why cells matter
Session 02

Cell energetics & chemical bonds

How a cell pays for everything it does. We cover metabolism end to end — catabolism, anabolism — and the three bonds that store and release the energy behind every one of those reactions.

  • Metabolism
  • Catabolism vs. anabolism
  • Hydrogen bonds
  • Covalent bonds
  • Ionic bonds
  • Polarity
Session 03

Cellular respiration & genetics

The most ground of the three. Glycolysis through the electron transport chain, ATP accounting included — then a turn into heredity: genes, alleles, and the 23 pairs you were handed at the start.

  • Glycolysis
  • Krebs cycle
  • Electron transport chain
  • ATP yield
  • Genes & alleles
  • Chromosomes

Session dates are announced on Instagram and through our links page.

Beyond the stethoscope

Healthcare needs more than doctors.

Some of the most important work in medicine is done by people who never applied to med school. We deliberately widen the definition, because a student who thinks there's only one door will walk away when it closes.

Computer science

Diagnostics, imaging models, health records, and the software every hospital runs on.

Engineering

Prosthetics, medical devices, biomaterials — the physical things that keep people alive.

Business & policy

Who gets care, at what price, and how a clinic stays open long enough to give it.

Public health

The work that treats a population rather than a patient — and usually gets there first.

Questions

Before you sign up

Who can join?

Any student with a strong interest in health care, regardless of prior education or experience. We're based in Mountain House, California, but our sessions are online and we work with students internationally.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. MedXplore is a nonprofit and every session, slide deck, and resource is free.

Do I need a science background?

No. Sessions are built to be followed by someone seeing the material for the first time, and questions mid-session are encouraged rather than tolerated.

Where do sessions happen?

Live over video call. You'll get a link before the session — all you need is a device and somewhere reasonably quiet.

What's a “batch”?

Each session runs twice, as two smaller batches. It keeps groups small enough to actually talk in, and it means one busy afternoon doesn't cost you the session.

I want to help. What do you need?

Student volunteers to teach, design, and run outreach — and professionals willing to speak or mentor. Email us at medxplore.mh@gmail.com and tell us what you'd like to do.
